01 · Overview
Most banking apps assume fast data, new phones and English.

The problem

People in towns like Greytown often run their money on cash, airtime and stokvels. Many banking apps feel risky and confusing, and they use a lot of mobile data.

The goal

Design a banking app a first-time user can use with confidence to send money, buy airtime, track spending and save with a stokvel.

Constraints

  • Low data and slow networks
  • Entry-level Android phones
  • Four languages
  • High fear of scams

02 · Define
Who we're designing for
TM
Thandi Mkhize
34 · Teacher's assistant · Greytown
"I send money to my gogo every month, but I'm scared I'll press the wrong thing and lose it."
Proto-persona · to be validated

Goals

  • Send money to family quickly and safely
  • Buy airtime and electricity without queues
  • See where her money goes each month
  • Keep paying into her stokvel

Frustrations

  • Apps eat her prepaid data
  • Hidden fees she only sees afterwards
  • Banking language is hard to follow
  • Scam SMSes make her distrust every message

Device and context

  • Entry-level Android phone, often low on storage
  • Prepaid data, mostly bought in small bundles
  • Speaks isiZulu at home and English at work

How might we…

  • make every fee visible before she pays?
  • help her feel safe at the moment of sending?
  • keep the app useful on a slow, costly connection?
  • bring her stokvel into the app without losing trust?

06 · Key decisions
Designed around trust, data and language

Lite mode

Turns off images and animations to save data. It's on by default for prepaid users.

Four languages

English, isiZulu, isiXhosa and Afrikaans, chosen on the very first screen.

Fees up front

Every fee shows before you confirm, written in plain words ("No fee: Kuhle to Kuhle is free").

Safety at the right moment

A scam warning appears on the confirm screen, exactly when it matters.

Hide balance

One tap hides the balance for privacy in taxis and queues.

Stokvel built in

A shared pot everyone can see. That openness builds the trust stokvels depend on.

Accessibility checklist

  • Text contrast of at least 4.5:1 (WCAG AA)
  • Touch targets of 44 × 44 px or larger
  • Colour is never the only signal: icons and labels too
  • Large 56 px buttons and a built-in number pad
  • Works with screen readers: every icon has a label

Usability test plan (next step)

5 participants from Umvoti · moderated, in person · think-aloud

  • Send R350 to a saved family member
  • Buy R29 of airtime
  • Find how much you spent on transport
  • Freeze your card
  • Check when your stokvel pays out

Measure: task success, time on task, errors and confidence (1–5).
